# Modes

Use this file to distinguish the four default scenes.

## 1. Work Chat

Use for WeChat or similar day-to-day work messaging:

- coordinating tasks
- asking for updates
- sending reminders
- confirming next steps
- resolving small misunderstandings

Tone target:

- natural
- friendly
- efficient
- readable in one pass

Avoid:

- email-like formality
- pressure-heavy reminders
- customer-service phrasing
- unnecessary opening and closing courtesies

## 2. Manager Communication

Use for communication with a boss, supervisor, or senior stakeholder:

- status updates
- requests for support
- explanations
- progress syncs
- asking for approval or direction

Tone target:

- respectful
- steady
- clear
- accountable

Avoid:

- sounding defensive
- sounding emotional
- sounding overly casual
- excessive self-lowering or flattery

## 3. Work Email

Use for formal or semi-formal written communication:

- internal email
- external email
- cross-team coordination
- confirmations and follow-ups
- requests that benefit from structure

Tone target:

- professional
- concise
- structured
- polite

Avoid:

- chatty filler
- long throat-clearing openings
- repeated gratitude lines
- vague asks without a clear action

## 4. Networking

Use for relationship-building communication:

- cold outreach
- warm introduction follow-up
- thank-you messages
- reconnecting
- requesting a short conversation

Tone target:

- warm
- sincere
- light
- interested but not pushy

Avoid:

- sounding transactional too early
- overselling yourself
- exaggerated praise
- pretending intimacy that has not been earned

## Boundary Notes

Choose `Manager Communication` over `Work Chat` whenever hierarchy shapes the tone.
Choose `Work Email` over `Manager Communication` when the format and structure matter more than the hierarchy.
Choose `Networking` when the primary goal is connection-building rather than task execution.
